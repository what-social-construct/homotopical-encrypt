\begin{abstract}
We study list decoding and mutual correlated agreement for constant-rate
Reed--Solomon codes over smooth evaluation domains.  The primary received-word
space is the two-fold interleaving
\(\mathcal C^{\equiv 2}\subseteq(\F^2)^n\).  We isolate three layers that are
often conflated: normalized distance of a received word from the interleaved
code, worst-case list size at that radius, and the fraction of affine
challenges for which a scalar projection appears close while its underlying
pair does not.  A field-valued two-coordinate plane is exactly an arbitrary
word in \((\F^2)^n\), and it is equivalently the base--direction pair of an
affine line of scalar received words.  This gives a lossless interface between
interleaved list decoding and mutual correlated agreement.

At count radius \(n-k\), a direct agreement-set injection and a matching
received-word construction prove
\[
  L_m\!\left(\mathcal C,\frac{n-k}{n}\right)=\binom nk
\]
for every positive interleaving width.  Thus
\(\binom nk\le\varepsilon|\F|\) is necessary and sufficient for the requested
fractional list target at the capacity endpoint.  A next-shell construction
then gives a complete three-regime phase split.  We instantiate the safe
regime with an explicit rate-\(1/4\) smooth family over
\(\mathbb F_{3^n}\). For pair mutual correlated agreement, an injective
compressed-certificate map gives the radius-independent bound
\[
  |\Bad_{\MCA}|\le\binom n{k+1}.
\]
The same explicit family satisfies both
\(\binom nk\le2^{-128}|\F|\) and
\(\binom n{k+1}\le2^{-128}|\F|\). Thus its list target holds at the
capacity endpoint and its pair-MCA error is at most \(2^{-128}\) at every
normalized radius.  We also define the exact bad-count spectrum
\(B_{\mathcal C}(r)\) and certify a sharp small-radius transition over the
following boundary field for 128-bit security:
\[
  q=2^{128}+192513\equiv1\pmod {4096},\qquad (n,k)=(4096,1024).
\]
For this smooth instance, \(B_{\mathcal C}(0)=1\) and
\(B_{\mathcal C}(1)\ge2\). Hence its greatest safe count radius at 128 bits is
zero, its first unsafe count radius is one, and its safe real-radius set has
supremum \(1/4096\), which is not attained.  The optimal list-decoding radius
in the intermediate field-size regime remains open.
\end{abstract}
